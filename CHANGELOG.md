# Changelog

The running version shows at the top of the probe window, at the end of the on-screen readout's first line,
in `ReShade.log` (`HUD-less probe 1.1.0 ... loaded`, plus `updated from version ...` when it changed),
in ReShade's add-on list and in the file's Properties > Details.

| Version | Step | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3.0 | release | **No more "force field" around the character** when zoomed out (thin things near the character no longer stand still in generated frames). **Camera from Factorio's sprites**: the camera's pan is read off the exact sprite positions instead of fitted to the picture. **Measurements in the readout**: where the camera came from, the motion fit per layer (lower is better), and where the in-between frames land (whole pixels or between). **Character tracking** no longer follows another character walking past (multiplayer, main menu demo). **Settings reorganised** into sections on both tabs. Developer tools: 5 s camera recordings (numbered, always saved). Finding: Factorio's frames are pixel-exact, and DLSS-G / FSR resampling between pixels causes the alt-mode icon blur - roadmap 1.4. Betas 1-9 below. |
| 1.3.0 | beta 9 | **Even steps removed; a readout for the module blur.** Beta 8's *Even out Factorio's pixel steps* did not help the module blur and left trails from earlier frames on quick direction changes: removed (the code is back to beta 7). New readout line: where the generated frames put the world (e.g. at 4x with a 7 px step: 1.75 / 3.50 / 5.25 px) and whether that lands on whole pixels. Beta 8: **Test: even out Factorio's pixel steps.** Factorio moves the world in whole pixels - running it steps 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 6 px per frame (6.9 on average), sideways and up/down alike - and frame generation faithfully fills in between those uneven real frames; on rows of identical alt-mode icons along the motion that shows as a pulse. With *Even out Factorio's pixel steps (test)* (*Motion vectors* tab, off by default) each frame's world is shown shifted by a fraction of a pixel (at most half; sharp Catmull-Rom resampling) so it moves by the average step every frame; the GUI and the character stay where Factorio drew them, and the motion vectors include the shift. Works with DLSS-G and FSR; needs *Camera from Factorio's sprites*; the readout shows its state. Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 7: **Settings reorganised; camera trace always saved.** *Frame generation* tab in sections: Character, Scene cuts, Display and overlays, Status, Setup (the GUI-capture warning moved to the top). *Motion vectors* tab: a Status section (camera source, motion fit, depth), the main switches, then collapsible Character tracking, Trains / cars / bots, Alt-mode icons (with the air split), Fine tuning, Debug views (flip sign moved there) and - with the developer tools - Recordings and measurements; snapshot-point details only with the developer tools. *Record 5 s of camera data* is saved by the game's present thread as soon as it is complete (it was only written while the Motion vectors tab was open, so it was often lost) and numbered (camera_trace_N.csv) instead of overwriting the last one. Beta 6: **Less work on Factorio's thread.** The beta 5 traces show every layer, alt-mode icons included, moving exactly with the camera (residual 0 in all 244 moving frames), so *Alt-mode icons: motion from their own sprites* changes nothing in vanilla; it is now off by default (kept for mods), and the per-layer measurement behind it - about 0.7 ms per frame on Factorio's render thread - only runs with that option on or while a camera trace is recorded. Beta 5: **Alt-mode icons: motion from their own sprites.** The beta 4 trace shows Factorio moves the world in whole pixels: running sideways it went 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 6 px per frame (6.9 on average), and the picture matched worse in exactly the 6-px frames - something on screen did not take that step with the world. Each world stage's sprites (floor, objects, air: alt-mode icons and bots) are now compared with the camera every frame, and alt-mode icons get the shift their own sprites made (*Motion vectors* tab: *Alt-mode icons: motion from their own sprites*, on). The camera trace also records, per stage, how its sprites moved relative to the camera. Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 4: **Finding the alt-mode pulse.** Since beta 2 all alt-mode icons pulse-blur while moving sideways (beta 1 did not). Beta 2 added two things that run every frame: the camera from the sprites and the motion fit pass; each can now be switched off on its own (*Motion vectors* tab: *Camera from Factorio's sprites*, *Measure the motion fit*). *Record 5 s of camera data* writes hudless_probe\camera_trace.csv: per frame the sprites' shift, the picture's fit and which one was used. Beta 3: **Test: alt-mode icons without a depth edge.** Beta 2 showed the camera was already right (the picture fit is 0.02 px from the sprites' exact shift) and the motion fit is as good as the camera's everywhere, so the module and science-pack rows that blur together with DLSS-G while moving sideways zoomed out are not wrong motion. Icons had their own, nearer depth: an edge around every icon, a few pixels apart zoomed out. They now get the depth of the building under them (*Motion vectors* tab: *Alt-mode icons: depth of the building under them*, on; off = as in 1.2). Motion fit shown with three decimals. Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 2: **Camera from Factorio's sprites; motion fit in the readout.** The camera's pan is now read off the exact positions of the sprites Factorio draws (static buildings all move by exactly the camera's shift) instead of being fitted to the picture to a fraction of a pixel - aimed at sharp repeating things that jittered or blurred together with DLSS-G (module rows, rails). Only when the sprites give it exactly; while zooming, and without *Character tracking*, the picture fit is used as before (*Motion vectors* tab: *Camera from Factorio's sprites*, on). The readout shows where the camera came from and how far the picture fit was from the sprites, and a new *motion fit* line: per layer, how much the previous frame moved by the motion vectors differs from the new one (lower is better), next to the camera alone. Character tracking: a known animation frame without the colour layer must sit where the camera is centred, like the colour layer (another character walking past - multiplayer, the main menu's demo - was followed off the centre). Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 1: **No more "force field" around the character.** Zoomed out, running moves the world by only 1-2 px per frame; inside a circle around the screen centre (the still zone, half the screen height across) thin, sharp things - elevated rails, power wires, building edges - matched "no motion" about as well as that small shift, kept zero motion and stood still in generated frames while the rest slid. With the exact character mask, nothing but the character stays put any more, at any camera speed. Motion shaders recompile once. Roadmap for 1.3-1.4 in docs/ROADMAP.md. |

The README is rewritten for players: why the add-on exists, what it does, install and first start, in short; the technical details and the full settings reference moved to docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md (also in the release zip). Draft release notes for 1.2.0. Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 35: Belt items slide again; fewer false scene cuts; stutter breakdown. Beta 34 gave all floor the camera's motion, so items on belts moved only at real frames (slight jitter at 4x, most visible running diagonally). A belt now keeps its own motion where it is certain (its block passed the repeat test, the motion is belt-like and the pixel clearly matches it); repeating surfaces and opposite neighbours still take the world's motion. Scene cuts need a mean difference of 0.08 (was 0.04: zooming, explosions and fast turns reset frame generation many times a minute). The stutter log also shows the bridge's wait for the GPU queue and the time inside Present. Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 34: Fewer stutters; belts and grass follow the world. The scene-cut check made Factorio's thread wait for the GPU every frame (about 4 ms, and 30-60 ms when the GPU was busy with generated frames - most stutters the add-on caused). It now waits at most 2 ms; when the GPU is not done by then the check is judged at the next frame (a cut found that late costs only the frames generated across it). Belts, rails, pipes, grass and decoratives (the floor layer) now always move with the world and get the ground's depth: beta 33 did not catch all of an empty belt's wrong motion (two belts side by side in opposite directions), and grass flickering between ground and floor in the layer view no longer flickers in depth. Items on belts cross-fade instead of sliding (a pixel or two per frame). *Motion vectors* tab: *Belts and other floor move with the world* (on). Motion shaders recompile once. Beta 33: Belts no longer stick and blur. An empty belt's scrolling surface repeats, so the motion search found it moving one way in some blocks and the other way in others, and kept that. Own motion on belts / floor is now only kept when no other shift along the same line matches nearly as well; otherwise the belt moves with the world (its surface cross-fades between frames). Bots and items on sparse belts still get their own motion. The motion shaders are recompiled once. Beta 32: Stutter counter, Steam overlay, multiplayer. The on-screen readout counts stutters (frames over twice the usual time) and shows the add-on's own time on Factorio's render thread; each stutter is logged with what took the time (the add-on's parts vs. outside it, and whether a mask dump, scene cut or multiplier change came with it) in ReShade.log and hudless_probe\stutters.txt (the previous session's kept as stutters_previous.txt). The Steam overlay opened unseen under the frame generation window: the window is now hidden while it is open (Steam's own notice, or Shift+Tab if Steam does not send one; settings on the main tab). Multiplayer: another player near the screen centre is no longer taken for your character (it must sit right where the camera is centred, and stay there while its colour is learned). Includes beta 31. Beta 31: Shadow while taking off / landing and over entities; readout. The shadow's darkness is measured separately for walking and flying, on ground and on objects (whether objects under it are darkened at all), and each pixel uses its own. A shadow the mask only held from an earlier frame (its place estimated, e.g. while taking off) is no longer laid over as a darkening (that could brighten the wrong spot): it is held as before for those frames. The on-screen readout (Ctrl+F11) shows the mask (character / shadow found, held, missing, counts over the last 10 s) and the measured darkness, so ReShade need not be opened. The motion shaders are recompiled once. Beta 30: The character's shadow in generated frames: laid over as a darkening. Until now its pixels were held still with whatever ground was under them, so at 4x that ground stood for three frames and jumped (noise, dragging). Now the add-on measures how dark the shadow is (where the camera moves the ground across its edge: the same ground point inside and outside it) and, once known, frame generation gets the ground without the shadow, moving with the world, and the shadow comes back as a darkening laid over every frame, staying put with the character - in both *laid over* and *generated* mode. Until it is measured (run around a little) the shadow is held as before. The laid-over character takes its outline from 2 % mask coverage and is grown by 1 px instead of 2 (less halo). *Motion vectors* tab: *Lay its shadow over as a darkening* (on by default) and the measured darkness. The motion shaders are recompiled once. Beta 29: Exact character mask, step 2: used by frame generation. The mask (now with a flying bit) is shared with the D3D12 side every frame; the motion pass no longer guesses the character from its motion: the character's and its shadow's pixels stay put on screen (zero motion, their depth), and nothing else is held ("stays put" outside the mask gets the camera's motion). The held character for the UI layer (laid over, painted over) comes from the mask too. Settings: *Character in generated frames: laid over / generated* (was *Keep the character sharp*); *Motion vectors* tab: *Use it for frame generation* (on by default) and what frame generation uses. The motion shaders are recompiled once (up to a minute). Beta 28: Character shadow: no longer disappears for single frames - when it is missed, the last one is held for up to 3 frames, moved with the body (status "held from last frame"); the window it must lie in is absolute (running to high flight) instead of around the learned flying place, which beta 27 rejected the real shadow with (flying height varies). Beta 27: Character shadow: tree shadows are no longer taken for it. Its learned place had drifted (it learned from a tree shadow, then preferred tree shadows); now every candidate must be the size of a character's shadow (0.6-2x the body wide, 0.25-1.1x high) and near its place whatever else speaks for it, the learned place is kept within what a character's shadow can be, and texture regions / per-frame places are only remembered from picks that fit the place. Beta 26: Character mask: the body may be up to 3x the colour layer's size (beta 25's 2x lost the character facing up or down, where the colour layer covers only the torso; trees are 4.2x and more); the shadow's place is interpolated between its running and flying place while taking off and landing, and the sideways tolerance is wider (it depends on the facing). Beta 25: Character mask next to trees: the body may be at most 4x the colour layer's area and 2x its size (hugging a tree, the tree - drawn right before the character and covering it - was taken for the body, and its shadow for the character's); a body found by a remembered frame must be about the character's size. Beta 24: Character shadow: each body animation frame remembers where its shadow sits (exact, so turns no longer depend on the average place); taking off and landing, the shadow may sit at either its running or its flying place (the body counts as flying while its shadow is still on the ground). Beta 23: Character shadow: also found by its place relative to the body (left edge, bottom edge, width, height in body sizes; learned separately running and flying, with defaults for running), so it no longer blinks when the character turns or drops while standing; a shadow with evidence (remembered, by its place) is no longer thrown out for jumping by about the camera shift. Status "shadow by its place"; the dump shows the learned place. Beta 22: Character mask: Factorio's quad index buffer has 32-bit indices (recognised now: the cheap path is used again); shadow sprites are read on every screen-sized target again (the shadow was seen on two different ones, so keeping to one made it flicker); a colour lock is released after 2 s without it (loading a save no longer keeps the main menu character's colour for 5 s); a dump is also written when only the shadow is lost (at most 6, 18 dumps in all). Beta 21: Character mask drops: Factorio's quad index buffer is recognised when the pattern holds only for its first part (beta 20 did not recognise it, so it never knew how full the vertex buffer was - "0 copied early", hundreds of draws lost, the camera shift without sprites); how full the buffer is also comes from the draws' own vertex ranges; after any lost draw, every draw's vertices are copied right at the draw for 10 s; camera sprites also from draws off the quad pattern. Index buffer detection logged in ReShade.log; the dump shows draws on the quad pattern. Beta 20: Character mask: vertices are copied before Factorio reuses its vertex buffer. With many sprites on screen (zoomed out, forests) the 1 MB buffer is filled and discarded several times a frame, so reading it at present gave the earlier draws' places with later sprites' data - random blocks in the overlay, the character dropped, the shadow and the camera shift never found. Now each draw's vertices are copied at Factorio's next no-overwrite map of the buffer, right at the draw when the buffer is nearly full, or at present. The colour layer must also be drawn right after a body (grey-tinted smoke and leaves were taken for it). Status shows draws and lost draws.
